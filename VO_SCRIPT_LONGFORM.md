## Script (timestamped to the 34-beat schedule)

### Chapter 1 — Cold Open

**[0:00–0:15]**
Some microphones are made to impress on a spec sheet. This one is made to disappear into your workflow — so what's left is simply the truth of the performance. This is the Neumann TLM 107, in full.

### Chapter 2 — Introduction

**[0:15–0:37] Meet the TLM 107**
The TLM 107 is a large-diaphragm studio condenser from Neumann Berlin — the flagship of the TLM range, and one of the most versatile microphones you can put in a modern recording setup. It's built for vocals, voiceover, instruments, and broadcast alike, without asking you to compromise on any of them.

**[0:37–0:57] Design philosophy**
Its design philosophy is simple: be the second microphone in the locker that quietly becomes the first choice. Flexible enough to handle almost any source you point it at, and straightforward enough that you never have to think twice about reaching for it.

**[0:57–1:15] What "TLM" means**
The name itself tells part of the story. TLM stands for Transformerless Microphone — Neumann's own transformerless output circuit, engineered to the same exacting standard as the rest of their range, at a more accessible point of entry.

### Chapter 3 — Neumann Berlin Heritage

**[1:15–1:40] Engineering heritage**
That standard comes from decades of history. For generations, Neumann microphones have shaped the sound of the world's most respected studios and broadcast rooms — a reputation built one session at a time, through consistency, precision, and an almost obsessive attention to sonic detail.

**[1:40–2:00] Reputation & standards**
Every TLM 107 carries that legacy forward. It's assembled and tested in Germany to Neumann's own internal quality standard — the same rigor applied across their entire studio microphone lineup, from compact broadcast booths to the largest scoring stages.

### Chapter 4 — Exterior Design & Finishes

**[2:00–2:20] The head grille**
You'll recognise it instantly by its head grille — a distinctive, box-shaped design that protects a large-diaphragm capsule tuned for clarity, detail, and depth. It's a shape that's as much a part of the Neumann identity as the sound itself.

**[2:20–2:40] Black & Nickel**
And it comes in two finishes. A deep matte black, or a classic nickel — your choice, purely aesthetic. Underneath either finish sits the exact same capsule, the exact same circuit, and the exact same honest, natural sound.

**[2:40–3:00] Craftsmanship close-up**
Look closer and you'll see why it holds up. From the knurled texture on the grille to the machined, gold-plated XLR connector, every surface is finished to a standard that's built to be handled — and to look right — for decades.

**[3:00–3:15] Badge & body detail**
Even the badge is enamel-finished metal, not a printed sticker. It's a small detail, but it says a great deal about the level of care that goes into building this microphone.

### Chapter 5 — Controls & Technical Deep Dive

**[3:15–3:31] Meet the control ring**
All of that control lives in one place — a single navigation switch on the body of the mic. No menus, no software, no computer required. Just a twist of the dial to set exactly how it listens.

**[3:31–3:41] Official control layout**
Here's that control ring up close — navigation switch, polar pattern selector, and low-cut filter, all laid out within easy reach of your thumb.

**[3:41–3:55] Polar pattern system**
That switch is what gives the TLM 107 its versatility. Polar pattern describes how a microphone hears the space around it — and this one gives you five patterns to choose from.

**[3:55–4:08] Omni & Wide Cardioid**
Omni picks up evenly from every direction, ideal for capturing room ambience or a natural group performance. Wide cardioid softens that focus slightly, for a more open pickup.

**[4:08–4:21] Cardioid & Hypercardioid**
Cardioid is the workhorse — rejecting sound from behind the mic while capturing your source in rich detail. Hypercardioid narrows that focus even further, isolating a single voice in a busier room.

**[4:21–4:32] Figure-8**
And figure-eight hears equally from the front and back, rejecting the sides entirely — perfect for duets, face-to-face interviews, or mid-side stereo work.

**[4:32–4:50] Pad & low-cut filter**
Alongside pattern selection, a switchable pad — minus six or minus twelve decibels — handles loud sources without distortion, and a low-cut filter tames rumble and proximity bass. Both are set right on the microphone, in seconds.

### Chapter 6 — Performance Specs

**[4:50–5:13] Self-noise**
Now, to the numbers that matter most. Self-noise is just ten decibels-A — genuinely, audibly quieter than the room you're likely recording in. For anyone chasing a clean, silent noise floor on a vocal or a voiceover take, that's an extraordinary starting point.

**[5:13–5:34] Max SPL & dynamic range**
And it doesn't just whisper — it can shout, too. The TLM 107 handles sound pressure levels up to one hundred forty-one decibels without breaking up, backed by a dynamic range of one hundred thirty-one decibels across its full frequency spectrum.

**[5:34–5:55] Frequency response character**
All of that adds up to a sound that stays flat, honest, and true. The frequency response is neutral through the midrange, with just a gentle presence lift above eight kilohertz — enough to add air and detail, without ever colouring the source.

### Chapter 7 — Accessories & Studio Set Contents

**[5:55–6:12] What's in the Studio Set**
So what actually comes in the Studio Set? Every TLM 107 Studio Set arrives complete — the microphone itself, its elastic suspension mount, and a wooden presentation case built to protect it for years.

**[6:12–6:26] The wooden case**
That case is a hinged hardwood box with brass clasps and a fitted foam interior — as much a piece of furniture as it is protection for transport and storage.

**[6:26–6:41] EA 4 shock mount**
The EA 4 elastic shock mount decouples the capsule from footfalls, desk bumps, and stand-borne vibration — keeping your recordings clean even in a less-than-perfect room.

**[6:41–6:56] Adapters & windscreen**
Round out the kit with thread adapters for different stand sizes, and a foam windscreen for plosive control — the small accessories that make daily use genuinely effortless.

**[6:56–7:10] Everything together**
Microphone, mount, and case — everything you need to start recording, together in one box, the moment you open it.

### Chapter 8 — Real-World Workflows

**[7:10–7:28] Vocals**
So where does all of this actually get used? Start with the voice. From an intimate lead vocal take to a fully polished voiceover session, the TLM 107 flatters every detail without ever turning harsh.

**[7:28–7:46] Voiceover & broadcast**
It's just as much at home in broadcast. Rich, present, and consistent take after take — exactly what a voiceover artist or a broadcaster needs from a main microphone, whether that's a studio booth or a converted bedroom.

**[7:46–8:04] Podcasting**
Podcasters get the same reliability. Whether it's mounted on a boom arm for hands-free recording, or sitting on a console alongside your other gear, the TLM 107 brings a broadcast-grade voice to any podcast setup.

**[8:04–8:20] Instruments**
And it isn't just for voices. Acoustic guitar, piano, strings, brass, drum overheads — the TLM 107 captures every instrument with the same natural, detailed character it brings to the spoken word.

**[8:20–8:34] Project & home studios**
Whether it's a first home setup or a growing project studio, this microphone earns its place on the stand — and keeps earning it as the room grows around it.

**[8:34–8:50] The wider studio ecosystem**
It also pairs naturally with the rest of the Neumann studio world — their own studio monitors and headphones — giving you a consistent, trustworthy signal chain from the microphone all the way to the mix.
